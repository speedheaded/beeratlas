Is it like Guinness?

No, and this is the most common disappointment in Prague. Guinness is dry, thin-bodied and poured with nitrogen; Kozel Černý is a sweet lager with caramel malt and normal carbonation. If you came for a stout, the closest thing on most Czech taps is a tmavý speciál at 13° — and you will usually have to leave the tourist centre to find one.

What the 10° means

It is not the alcohol. Degrees on a Czech menu are degrees Plato — how much malt sugar was in the wort before fermentation. Roughly, the alcohol lands near a third of the number: a 10° is about 3.8 %, a 12° about 4.4 %.
