Czech Pilsner from Source

A 20-litre recipe for the beer every pale lager is a copy of — built from the ingredients Plzeň actually uses, bought where they are grown and malted.

Most “Bohemian Pilsner” recipes are a German pilsner with a Czech label. The gap is not the malt bill — it is soft water, a decoction rest, generous low-alpha hops and six weeks of cold. Below: the target profile of Pilsner Urquell, the numbers to hit it in a 20 L batch, and the exact shopping list, priced.

That 71 % attenuation is the part people miss. Fermentation is stopped early and roughly 3.5 °P of malt stays in the beer. That unfermented sugar is what you read as body — it is why a 4.4 % Czech lager tastes fuller than a 5 % international one.

Hop character runs 0 to 10, where 0 is the Saaz end — herbal, earthy, faintly floral — and 10 is New World citrus and tropical fruit. A Czech pilsner sits at 1. Everything about the hop schedule below serves that number, and it is the single axis a substitute hop will move.

The four things that make it Czech

Water so soft it barely counts

Plzeň water is close to distilled — very low in sulphate and carbonate. That is why the bitterness lands clean instead of harsh. If your tap water is hard, cut it with distilled or RO water; this matters more than any other single decision on the page.

Decoction, or a very long single rest

Plzeň still runs a triple decoction: three portions of the mash are pulled, boiled and returned. Almost nobody does it at scale any more, and it is what gives the malt its depth without any specialty grain. If you are not set up for it, a single infusion at 65 °C for 75 minutes gets you most of the way — you will lose some richness and no one will call the beer wrong.

Saaz, used generously

Žatecký poloraný červeňák carries only 3.5 % alpha acid, so a Czech pilsner takes three to four times the hop weight of a modern IPA to reach half its bitterness. The bitterness lingers without ever turning resinous. A high-alpha substitute hits the IBU number and misses the beer.

Six weeks of cold

Lagering is the step industrial brewing cuts first, and the step you cannot fake. Three to six weeks near 2 °C clears the beer, softens the bitterness and carbonates it naturally. Bottle it at three weeks and you have made a green pilsner, which tastes exactly like it sounds.

Recipe · 20 litres into the fermenter

Grain

4.5 kg Czech Pilsner malt, 3–4.9 EBC, and nothing else. Assumes 75 % brewhouse efficiency — scale to yours. A single-malt grist is the hardest kind of beer to hide anything in, which is the point.

IBU by Tinseth at 3.5 % alpha, 20 L, OG 1.048. No dry hop — a Czech pilsner does not have one.

Yeast and fermentation

22 g dry lager yeast — two 11 g sachets, which is the correct pitch for 20 L of 12 °P wort at lager temperature. Under-pitching a lager is the most common way this beer goes wrong. Ferment at 10–12 °C, then drop to 2 °C for lagering.

Grain to glass · 42 days

1Day

Mashing

Crushed malt meets hot water and the grain's own enzymes cut its starch into fermentable sugar. The result is wort: sweet, hopless, not yet beer.

Triple decoction at Plzeň. Single infusion at 65 °C for 75 minutes is the accessible substitute.

Same day

Boil and hops

Ninety minutes, uncovered — a long boil drives off DMS, which pilsner malt produces more of than most. Hops go in on the schedule above. Nothing else in the process adds bitterness or aroma.

Firmly hopped by international standards and unremarkable by Czech ones.

6–10Days

Fermentation

Cool to 10–12 °C and pitch both sachets. Sugar becomes alcohol and CO₂; cold and slow keeps the profile clean. Plzeň historically fermented open, in sandstone cellars, and still runs a small parallel batch that way for comparison.

Stop near 71 % attenuation. Let it run dry and you have made a German pilsner.

21–45Days

Lagering

Rest cold, near 2 °C. The beer clears, softens, carbonates naturally and loses the harshness of young beer. Three weeks is the floor; six is better.

Finish

Packaging

Plzeň filters for bottle and can, and delivers tank beer unfiltered and unpasteurised. At home, skip both — unfiltered and unpasteurised is the version worth flying to Plzeň for, and you can just make it.
